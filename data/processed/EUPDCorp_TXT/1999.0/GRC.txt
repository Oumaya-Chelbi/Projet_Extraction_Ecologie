the rapporteur. - (el) mr president, it is truly sad that at the dawn of the 20th century, a century marked positively by the peaceful revolution of women, there are still countries like kuwait or afghanistan where half the population - women - are deprived of the most basic human rights. in the case of kuwait, what happened at the end of november, when the national parliament rejected by 32 votes to 30 a bill to grant women the right to vote and stand for election, is not just a violation of the most basic rights of women and a revolting political act but also, paradoxically, a violation of the very constitution of kuwait, article 29 of which states that all citizens are equal before the law, regardless of their gender, religion, race or ethnicity. in addition, kuwait is a signatory to the un international convention on the elimination of all forms of discrimination against women. it seems that the members of the legislature - all men, of course - are bound neither by the country's constitution, nor by the laws, nor by their international obligations, nor by the political will of the country's leaders who tabled the bill in question in parliament. but this decision, apart from the medieval misogyny it evokes and its profoundly anti-democratic character, is a very clear warning against any attempt to modernise and democratize the country. that is why the european parliament must today send a strong message of sympathy and solidarity to the women of kuwait who are fighting for their fundamental rights. we also call on the representative of the kuwaiti national assembly to do everything possible to ensure that the issue is reviewed and justice is done to women, so that in the next elections in 2003 women in that country will enjoy all their political rights, starting with the right to vote and to stand for election.
