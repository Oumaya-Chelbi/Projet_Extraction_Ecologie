in writing. - for reasons of principle as well as practical reasons, i was obliged to vote against the amendments to the text proposed by the commission. the proper functioning of economic and monetary union does not, in principle, require harmonisation of vat rates. in the united states, which is a perfect economic and monetary union, there are considerable tax differences between states. in practice, amendments nos 2 and 3 will have the effect of harmonising vat rates upwards. the only question is how long the minimum rate will be in force. the maximum rate isn't even mentioned. in the interests of price stability and the protection of consumer purchasing power, the opposite approach would be necessary: namely, the maintenance of the minimum rate of 15% for an indefinite period, accompanied by a voluntary and gradual reduction of the higher rates.
in writing. - i abstained in the final vote on the otherwise excellent report by mrs béatrice patrie because i consider it a political and scientific abdication to refer to the precautionary principle even on the basis of 'incomplete, inconclusive or uncertain scientific information' or even 'in the event of a low risk'. since there is no zero risk, scientists or at least policy makers must apply the criterion of proportionality, assess uncertain risks and assume their responsibilities. it is clear that citizens must be fully informed in all cases.

the compulsory age limit and also the compulsory early retirement are discriminatory. in this context, the commission is even invited to draw up a proposal for a directive aimed at combating discrimination on grounds of age. we must of course be careful not to contradict ourselves in the many long resolutions we adopt here. this means that on the one hand we want to get rid of the old by means of compulsory age limits in order to create jobs for the young, but on the other hand we are practically demanding the abolition of the compulsory age limit. i do not necessarily want all women to be in the workforce either, because we are calling for family and tax policies to increase women's participation in the workforce. i do not think that all women and men should have to work if they work. however, i am glad, and mrs flemming has pointed out this discrimination, that this discrimination against women no longer exists. when i started working for the unions, they told me i'd be fired if i got married. i'm still a bachelor today.
